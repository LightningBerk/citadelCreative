Frequently Asked Questions

What exactly is Citadel — a file, an app, or software?

Citadel is software — specifically a control layer that sits between your notes and your AI. In the current beta, that layer is implemented through a sophisticated context protocol (a structured configuration file) plus an MCP connection that gives your AI direct access to your vault. This is the working core of the system. As we develop Citadel further with beta tester input, it will grow into a full UI-driven application. The founding price reflects where we are now; the product will reflect where we're going.

Which note-taking apps and AI models does it support?

The beta works with Obsidian (our primary integration) and Claude (Desktop or Code). Broader integrations — Notion, Roam, Logseq, and support for GPT-4 and Gemini — are on the active roadmap and are exactly what founding tester feedback will help us prioritize and shape. If you have a strong preference for a specific stack, join and tell us — that's the whole point.

Do I need to know how to code?

No. If you can follow a setup guide, you can get Citadel running. We document every step — including the MCP configuration, which trips people up on their own. As a beta tester with direct Discord access, you also have Asa available if you hit something the guide doesn't cover. We want to know about those gaps — that's how the guide improves.

What does "founding tester" actually mean?

It means your experience with the product directly influences what Citadel becomes. When you run into friction, we want to know about it — that's a missing feature or a broken flow that gets added to the backlog. Founding testers vote on what gets built next. Your use cases become our use cases. We can only build software that handles the real world if real people are using it in the real world.

Is there a monthly subscription?

Not for founding testers. $20, once. You'll need a Claude.ai account (free tier works for getting started) and Obsidian is free for personal use. When Citadel grows into full software and supports multiple integrations, the pricing model will evolve — but your founding tester access is permanent and grandfathered.

What does "beta" mean in practice?

The core system works — we use it every day. "Beta" means the broader software vision (multi-app integrations, UI, additional AI models) is still being built, and the founding tester cohort is how we build it well. You're not getting something broken. You're getting something real at the stage where your input still shapes the outcome. That's a fundamentally different position than buying finished software off a shelf.
